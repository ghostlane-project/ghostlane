# Funding

Ghostlane and olcRTC are free software. Nobody has to pay to use them, and no
feature is held back for people who do not.

Development has been unfunded so far. This document says how funding will work
if the project is accepted by [Open Source Collective](https://opencollective.com/),
so that anyone deciding whether to give money can see where it goes first.

## Who holds the money

Open Source Collective, a 501(c)(6) non-profit in the United States, would act
as **fiscal host** for the project. It holds the funds, handles compliance and
accounting, and pays out approved expenses. The project itself is not a company
and has no bank account of its own.

Every contribution and every expense on an Open Source Collective budget is
public, itemised and visible to anyone, including people who have never given a
cent.

## What the money is for

In rough order of expected spending:

- **Development and maintenance.** Building features, fixing bugs, reviewing
  contributions, keeping dependencies current, and the unglamorous work of
  release engineering.
- **Infrastructure.** Build machines, the servers the release gate exercises,
  relays used for testing, code signing and notarisation costs, and developer
  program fees where they are needed to publish.
- **Testing.** Devices, network access in the places this software is meant to
  work, and paid services used to reproduce reported failures.
- **Security.** Audits, dependency review, and rewards for responsibly reported
  vulnerabilities when the budget allows it.
- **Documentation and translation.**

## Paying people

**Maintainers and contributors may be compensated from project funds for actual
work on the project.** This includes the primary maintainer. It is ordinary
practice on Open Source Collective and it is stated here rather than left
implicit.

The rules we hold ourselves to:

- An expense names the work it pays for and links to it: a pull request, an
  issue, a release, an invoice.
- Work is paid after it exists and can be inspected, not before.
- The same rules apply to the primary maintainer as to anyone else. A larger or
  recurring expense to a maintainer is announced on the Collective before it is
  filed, so that anyone funding the project can object.
- No payment is ever conditioned on a contribution being merged, and no
  contributor is required to accept payment.

## What the money is not for

- It does not buy influence over the roadmap. Funding a project does not
  purchase a feature, a merge, or a place in the release notes beyond the
  ordinary thanks any contributor gets.
- It does not fund anything closed. Work paid from project funds lands in these
  repositories under their open-source licences.

## Globvent Inc. is a separate thing

Ghostlane is published on the App Store and on Google Play by **Globvent Inc.**,
which owns and operates those developer accounts. A person or a company has to
hold a developer account for a mobile application to be distributed at all; that
is what Globvent Inc. does here, and it is the only role it has in this project.

To be explicit, because this is exactly the sort of thing a funder is right to
ask about:

- Project funds raised through Open Source Collective are **not** received by,
  held by, controlled by, or paid to Globvent Inc.
- Publication of the applications by Globvent Inc. gives it no claim on the
  project's funds and no control over how they are spent.
- The two do not share a treasury. Open Source Collective holds the project
  funds; Globvent Inc. holds the store accounts.
- Store fees paid to Apple and Google for those accounts are Globvent Inc.'s
  own cost today. If the project ever reimburses such a cost from its funds, it
  will be an ordinary itemised expense, visible like every other.

The source code stays under its open-source licences whatever happens to either
entity, and anyone may build and distribute their own copy under those licences.
